The salt appears every year on the morning of the drowning, a thin white line across the keeper's step, laid down in the dark by hands that are never seen; the old families leave it, and the old families say nothing about it, and the village has learned not to ask.

It is meant, so the story goes, to remind the sea of what it owes, though what the sea owes and to whom has never been settled, and the arguments about it in the inn have outlasted three landlords and a great many bottles.

She found the line unbroken that morning, sharp and careful against the wet stone, and she stood over it for a long time with her hands in her pockets, thinking of the man who had taught her the lamp, and of the rope, and of the night the quay broke beneath him.

Then she stepped carefully over the salt, as one steps over a sleeping animal, and went up the long stair to the lamp room, where the clockwork was waiting and the morning light lay pale and slanting on the brass.
