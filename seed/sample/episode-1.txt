SCENE 1 - THE LAMP ROOM, SALTMARSH LIGHT. DUSK.

[SFX: wind worrying the glass; the slow tick of the lamp's clockwork]

NARRATOR: Every evening, Mirela Voss climbs one hundred and twelve steps to wake the light. She counts them the way her mother counted them, out loud, so the stairwell knows she is coming.

MIRELA: One hundred and ten. Eleven. Twelve. Evening, old friend.

[SFX: a brass key turning; clockwork catching]

NARRATOR: Below her, Kessock Harbour folds itself away for the night. Down on the quay, Sorrel Pike lifts the rope of the curfew bell.

[SFX: the curfew bell, three slow strokes, far below]

MIRELA: There it goes. Harbour shut, boats in, everyone safe and bored.

SCENE 2 - THE RADIO SHED. NIGHT.

[SFX: valves humming; a kettle starting to sing]

ILSABET: Come in, come in, mind Keats, he's warm.

MIRELA: You named a valve Keats.

ILSABET: I name every valve after a dead poet. They last longer if they feel appreciated. Tea?

MIRELA: Ilsabet, last night, on the old channel. One point seven. Did you hear anything?

ILSABET: The old channel's empty. Has been since the Long Night.

MIRELA: It wasn't empty at three o'clock.

[SFX: a dial turning slowly through static]

SCENE 3 - THE LAMP ROOM. LATER.

[SFX: static, then a voice beneath it, patient and close]

THE LISTENER: Mirela. Your mother used to sing on these stairs.

MIRELA: Who is this? Identify yourself.

THE LISTENER: She sang the one about the gannet and the bell. You never learned the second verse.

[SFX: the static swallows the voice]

NARRATOR: Mirela stands very still in the lamp room, her grey eyes on the dark water, and does not move until the clockwork needs winding.

SCENE 4 - THE QUAY. MORNING.

[SFX: gulls; boots on wet stone]

WREN: Morning, Keeper. Three pieces of green glass for one question.

MIRELA: The rate was two pieces yesterday.

WREN: Inflation. Did you hear the ghost?

MIRELA: There is no ghost, Wren.

WREN: Then why are you awake?

[SFX: the Gannet's engine coughing in the distance]

TEODOR: Mira! Captain Marr says if I'm late again she'll feed me to the crabs.

MIRELA: Then go, Teo. And stay on the boat this time.

TEODOR: I always stay on the boat. The boat is where the water isn't.

NARRATOR: The Gannet pulls away. On the rail, Teodor holds on with both hands, the way he has since he was small, and does not look down.
